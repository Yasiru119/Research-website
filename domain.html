1. Literature Survey

An extensive review of automotive technical instruction and digital pedagogical systems reveals three prevailing educational paradigms:

Instructional Paradigm Current Strengths Observed Limitations in Practical Training Key Citations
Traditional Manuals & 1-on-1 Mentorship Direct tactile feedback, high instructor accuracy, established vocational accreditation. Severe instructor scalability limits (>1:10 trainee ratio), divided visual attention looking away from the engine bay, and late hazard intervention. Brooke [4], ISO 26262 [5]
Passive Digital Courseware (2D / Video) Scales to arbitrary student cohort sizes, accessible on budget consumer hardware. Lack of spatial 3D context, inability to verify physical execution or torque specifications, and absence of active hazard mitigation. Radu [6]
Enterprise Optical Headsets (HMDs) Hands-free immersion, high-precision spatial SLAM anchors, advanced telepresence. Prohibitive hardware expenditure ($3,500+ per unit), fragile hardware unsuitable for grease/heat, and heavy dependency on high-bandwidth cloud infrastructure. Sandler et al. [1], TFLite [2]

Theoretical Synthesis

The proposed AutoLearnAR framework addresses these trade-offs by utilizing quantized convolutional networks (MobileNetV2 [1]) on embedded edge hardware (TensorFlow Lite [2]) via the Android CameraX pipeline [3]. Practical viability is verified using standardized usability scales (SUS [4]) and functional safety containment principles aligned with automotive standards [5].

2. Research Gap

While augmented reality has been implemented in dedicated industrial manufacturing plants, there exists a critical gap in offline, smartphone-based digital supervisors capable of autonomously guiding, verifying physical steps, and grading novice technicians on commodity mobile hardware without external servers.

3. Research Problem Formulation

Automotive vocational training lacks an objective, proactive supervisory mechanism that scales without inflating instructor costs or compromising student safety. Instructors cannot simultaneously monitor multiple trainees working on active engines, leading to subjective evaluation and delayed hazard interception.

This central challenge is divided into four interdependent subproblems:

Subproblem 01

Markerless Visual Context

Classifying tightly packed and occluded engine parts without physical QR or fiducial markers under non-uniform workshop lighting.

Subproblem 02

Procedural Task Mapping

Mapping identified mechanical components to ordered assembly graphs and tracking student progression across state transitions.

Subproblem 03

Spatial AR Step Verification

Projecting 3D directional cues onto physical engine components and verifying completion through a smartphone display without headsets.

Subproblem 04

Automated Safety & Scoring

Detecting tool interactions in hazardous proximity zones and calculating an objective 100-point performance score from execution telemetry.

5. Research Methodology

The development followed an agile, component-driven engineering methodology executed over four bi-weekly sprints. Testing was conducted on a physical Suzuki Alto 800L vehicle testbed.

End-to-End Execution Sequence

  1. Image Frame Acquisition: CameraX captures continuous camera frames at 30 FPS with zero memory duplication.
  2. Edge Machine Learning Inference: Quantized MobileNetV2 (TFLite) predicts component classification and passes validated componentId.
  3. Workflow Instantiation: State manager retrieves the corresponding task graph and establishes active sub-step targets.
  4. Spatial 3D Projection: The AR Canvas projects directional vectors and highlights target regions while Android TextToSpeech generates audio instructions.
  5. Temporal Hold Verification: A continuous 3-second visual fixation window confirms step completion without screen taps.
  6. Safety Interception: The safety evaluation engine evaluates tool proximity and spatial forbidden zones, triggering urgent audio/visual alerts (~120ms).
  7. Performance Synthesis: Telemetry metrics (completion latency, error counts, retry penalties) are synthesized into a 100-point score stored locally in SharedPreferences/JSON.
